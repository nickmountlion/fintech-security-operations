Building an in-house security function for a fintech

A specialty-finance company of about 130 people moved security away from an outside IT provider. For six months I led the consulting team that ran it day to day and reported to executives and shareholders. This is how the work was organized and what it changed.

Context

A US specialty-finance company runs on Microsoft 365 with a separate identity provider and a mixed macOS and Windows fleet. It had relied on an outside IT provider for security and wanted the function in-house.

My role was Lead Cybersecurity Infrastructure Architect and Engineer, through a consultancy. I owned planning, prioritization and security reporting to executive leadership and shareholders.

Attribution: I led the team. Engineers on the team executed much of the hands-on work listed here, and I say "we" where that is the case.

First 30 days

  1. Environment auditInventory of identities, endpoints, applications and admin paths, so priorities came from evidence.
  2. Enterprise browserArchitecture for a managed browser as a policy enforcement point.
  3. MDM migrationPlan to consolidate device management onto one platform per operating system.
  4. SOC 2 readinessMapped controls to evidence and to named owners.
  5. Privileged accessHardening of admin roles and the paths used to reach them.

How I ran the function

Daily sync, owners and dates

Thirty minutes every morning. Every item had one owner and a date, and blocked items were escalated the same day.

One tracker per initiative

Applications, users, roles and status in a sheet the client could read without a meeting.

Reports for executives

Status written as decisions, risk and cost, with the technical detail attached, not leading.

Written approval first

Anything that could touch the business got written sign-off before the change, and minutes after every meeting.
